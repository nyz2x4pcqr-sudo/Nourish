## 🦴 Roasted bone marrow is a starter, not a lunch
0.1.12 planned roasted bone marrow from a cookbook as a Monday lunch: 434 kcal and 46 g protein, with 2 oz of chicken breast added for protein, and a description that was its ingredient list, a gallon of brine water included. Everything behind that is fixed:
- **"Beef marrow bones" were read as beef steak**, 4 pounds of bones and all. Bone marrow is now its own food (almost pure fat), and only the marrow inside the bones counts.
- **Starters, dips, spreads and sides are never meals:** bone marrow, pâté, crostini, hummus, a recipe that says "serve as a side", and any dish with hardly any protein (under 8% of its calories). "Lava Cakes" is a dessert; green beans aren't beans.
- **Nothing is added to make a dish pass.** A dish that isn't a meal on its own is never given extra protein. A real meal that's a little short gets *a bit more of its own* protein ("a bit more chicken thigh"), never a different meat on the side. What's added is never more than the dish's own protein, and plant dishes get plant protein.

## ⚖️ Nutrition that counts what you eat
- **Bones and shells don't count:** ribs, short ribs, wings, drumsticks, whole chickens, shanks, oxtail, T-bones, shell-on shrimp, mussels and clams count only their edible part.
- **What's thrown away doesn't count:** a brine (found from the steps when the book doesn't say), soaking water, the pot of frying oil (only what the food takes up, about a tablespoon a serving), and the flour, egg and crumbs left over after breading.
- **Real weights:** a prawn is about 15 g (not 100), a fish fillet about 150 g, a boneless chicken thigh about 115 g, a cup of chopped chicken about 140 g, a drained can of beans about 60% of the can. "4 4-ounce fillets", "2 x 180g fillets", "(about 4 pounds)", "2 cups (200g) cheese" and "1/2 - 1 tsp" are all read properly.
- **Cooked pasta, noodles and rice** count as cooked, not dry ("1 pack ready-cooked noodles" was 1,075 kcal).
- **The right food:** "vanilla yogurt" is yogurt (not vanilla extract), "nut or seed butter" is a nut butter (not dairy butter), "pumpkin, chia or sunflower seeds" is seeds (not squash). New in the table: ribs, short ribs, oxtail, lobster, clams, tomatillos, broad beans, cooked grains, acai.
- **"Butter lettuce" is not butter.** The olive-oil-for-butter rule had turned a head of lettuce into half a head of olive oil: a 2,900 kcal lunch.
- A website's own nutrition is used only when its numbers add up.

## 🍽️ Plans that make sense
- **Cookable portions:** ½, ¾, 1, 1¼… never "1.31 servings".
- **"Involved" dishes are for dinner** (or a meal you've set to no rush). A side the app adds doesn't make a lunch count as more work.
- **Days stay on target:** after protein and fiber are added, the day is sized again (0.1.12 left some days 15–30% over). No empty dinners on high-calorie days. No meal is planned at over 100 g of protein (a recipe with 4 chicken thighs a person had been planned as 6 thighs).
- **At most 3 meals a week from one site or book, really:** swaps for calories or vitamins could add a 4th.
- **Descriptions say what the dish is**, e.g. "Roasted chicken thighs and new potatoes with lemon and garlic. Ready in about 45 minutes." Never brine, frying oil, amounts or units. A website's own description is kept only where it describes the dish (no "Recipe video above.", no foods the recipe doesn't have).
- When a day still ends up well short of protein (it can happen on a vegan plan), the Plan screen says so plainly and suggests a protein snack.

## 🛡️ Allergies and diets
- **Plain protein powder is whey (milk):** never added for vegans or a dairy allergy. Pea, soy, rice, hemp or plant protein is fine.
- **A tree-nut allergy now catches nut butter, mixed nuts and pesto.**
- Avoiding olives no longer rules out everything cooked in olive oil, and olive oil is never swapped in for someone who avoids it.

## 📚 Cookbooks
- **PDF cookbooks keep their recipes:** "Ingredients", "Method" and "Serves 4" appear on most pages, and were being removed as if they were the book's running header, so recipes lost their titles and servings. A step repeated at the bottom of pages is kept too.
- **A title set over two lines** ("Sheet-Pan Sausage and Peppers with" / "Crispy Potatoes") is one title.
- **"For the brine" and "For frying" groups** label their lines, and **"To serve"** inside an ingredient list is a group, not the method.
- A scanned PDF on a PC says it looks scanned and that the iPhone app can read it.

## 🍌 Add food: logged foods are kept
- The activity log and the food log were saved in the same place, so anything logged with **Add food** was overwritten half a second later and gone after a restart. They're apart now. With Nourish on your PC, what the PC kept comes back the first time you open this version.

## 🔍 Plan audit (run before this release)
A new tool, `tools/audit/plan-audit.js`, makes 25 full 7-day plans the way the app does and checks every meal (whether it's really a meal, the right slot, believable nutrition for its main food, added ingredients, thrown-away ingredients, bones and shells, repeats and sources, luxury ingredients, descriptions, titles, portions, daily totals, allergies and avoided foods, and Nourish's own recipes). It uses three test cookbooks (an EPUB, a text PDF and a scanned PDF) and 587 real web recipes, with goals Lose, Maintain and Gain, 1,500–3,400 kcal, vegetarian, pescatarian and vegan, nut, shellfish, dairy and gluten allergies, budgets, a busy schedule, cooking for two, snacks and a weekly budget.
