## 🥗 Meals built on nutrition science
- **Protein follows your body weight.** Add your weight in Settings → Profile and protein is set to 1.6 g per kg a day, or 2.0 g per kg when your goal is to lose weight. Without a weight, your own protein target is used, as before.
- **Protein in every meal:**
  - Every main meal has 25–40 g, and **breakfast is never under 25 g**.
  - When a meal falls short, Nourish adds a protein food and works its numbers out again. For example: protein powder or Greek yogurt in a smoothie or oats, more eggs with an egg breakfast, lean chicken, tofu or tuna at lunch or dinner. It never adds something you avoid.
  - Snacks always have some protein.
  - The recipe says what was added.
- **Fiber:** at least 30 g a day. A day short of it gets chia seeds, berries, broccoli or chickpeas added.
- **Expensive ingredients are for dinner only.** Steak cuts, lobster, scallops, lamb and the like never appear at breakfast, lunch or as a snack.
- **Fills you up:** lunch and dinner under about 1.5 calories per gram come first, more so when you're losing weight.
- **Carbs and fat** aren't forced low. There's a new choice in Profile: Balanced (the default), Lower-carb or Lower-fat.
- **Better fats:** olive oil instead of butter or lard for cooking, where the dish allows it (not baking or butter sauces). You can switch this off.
- **Fatty fish about once a week** (salmon, trout, mackerel, sardines), unless your diet or dislikes rule it out.
- **Whole foods first:** recipes made from whole ingredients are preferred over processed shortcuts. Protein powder is fine.
- **Vitamins and minerals:**
  - Each week's vitamin D, calcium, potassium and magnesium are estimated (the nutrition table now has these and fiber, from USDA).
  - When the week is low in one, a better meal is swapped in, or the Plan screen says so in plain words.
- **"Front-load my day"** (Profile → Calories by meal) puts more of the day's calories at breakfast and lunch. Bigger dinner stays the default.
- **Fixed:** "2 bay leaves" were counted as 200 g, about 600 extra calories in some soups. "Salt pork" was counted as plain salt.

## 📅 A weekly calorie budget
- **Settings → Profile → Count calories: Daily or Weekly.**
- **Weekly mode:**
  - The week's budget is seven normal days.
  - Add **big days** (for example Saturday at 3,200 kcal). The other days share what's left evenly.
- **No day goes under 75% of a normal day.** If your big days would need that, Nourish shows each day and says plainly how far over the week would go. They're only used once you tap **Use these days anyway**.
- Protein and fiber targets stay the same **every** day, including lighter days.
- The Plan screen shows **your week**: the budget, what's used, what's left, and each day's calories.

## 🔪 Technique books
- **Drop cooking-technique books** (EPUB or PDF) into Recipe Books, just like cookbooks.
- **How they're read:**
  - Nourish tells whether a book is mostly recipes or mostly technique. You can change that on the book's row in Settings → Recipes.
  - A technique book is read once, in the background. If the app is closed part-way, reading carries on where it stopped.
  - The book becomes short **technique notes**: what to do and why, in 1–3 sentences. They're tagged by ingredient, cooking method and kind of dish, and stored offline with the book's title.
  - Pages that are only pictures are skipped and noted in the log.
- **How they're used:** when the AI writes or changes a recipe, it gets the 3–5 notes that match the dish, so its steps use the right technique.
- **What you see:**
  - Settings → Recipes shows how far a book has got and how many notes it has found.
  - My books lists each technique book's notes.

## 📝 In the activity log
- Every rule that fires is logged so you can send it to me: protein or fiber added (where, and why), oil swapped in, fatty fish, the week's vitamins and minerals, the weekly calories per day, technique notes used, and why Nourish's own recipes were used for a meal (what turned the web recipes away).

## 📕 PDF cookbooks, including scanned ones
- **Scanned books are read on iPhone.** A scanned PDF is just photos of pages, with no text inside. Nourish now reads those pages with the iPhone's own text recognition (Apple Vision, on the phone, nothing is sent anywhere).
  - Pages are read a few at a time, in the background, so you can keep using the app.
  - **Pause** keeps your place and **Carry on** continues from the same page. **Stop** ends the reading.
  - When the phone gets hot, Nourish waits for it to cool down.
- **Pages in two columns** (ingredients on the left, steps on the right) are read one column after the other.
- **Older cookbook layouts are recognised.** That means recipes without "Ingredients" or "Method" headings:
  - titles in capitals, with the English name in brackets;
  - ingredients in lettered groups ("A", "B");
  - numbered steps;
  - several recipes run together on one page.
- Running heads (the book's name on every page), page numbers and words split at the end of a line are cleaned up. Each recipe keeps its **page number**.
- The book's own **title and author** are used (from the file's details), without tags like "(Z-Library)".

## 📗 EPUB books that imported nothing
- **Every chapter is read, in the book's order.** Some books store their chapter files in a way Nourish couldn't find, for example made on Windows, or with different capitals. Those chapters were skipped without a word.
- **More recipe layouts are recognised:**
  - titles in bold instead of headings;
  - ingredient lists split by line breaks or set in tables;
  - a title spread over two lines;
  - short recipes like syrups.
- **Drinks, desserts, sauces and toppings** are kept with their own type. They're for browsing, snacks and chat, not for meal slots.
- **A book never finishes silently with 0 recipes.** Nourish says why, in plain words, for example "Read 42 chapters, but none of them has an ingredient list with amounts".

## 📚 Each book is read once
- **Why books were read again:** Nourish only wrote down what it had read after *every* book was finished, and only in the app's small settings storage. If the app closed first, or that storage was full, it started over.
- **Now:**
  - Every file is recorded in the recipe database, by its contents, as soon as it's read. That includes books with no recipes.
  - It's read again only when the file changes, or when you tap **Read again**.
  - Opening the books screen or checking the folder never reads anything again.
- **My books** shows, per book:
  - the recipes found;
  - how many are meals and how many are other kinds;
  - how many need review;
  - a **Read again** button.
- **Recipes that need review are easy to fix.** They're listed first, with what's missing ("the title looks cut off", "no steps"). **Fix it** opens the recipe to edit, and **Looks right** confirms it as it is.
- Titles that look cut off (starting with "in", "with", a small letter…) are marked for review. Titles set over two lines are joined.

## 🌐 Recipe sites
- **Allrecipes and HelloFresh** are not used. They were checked and left out on purpose: their terms of use forbid automated apps. Nourish doesn't try to get around that.
  - Before, they simply never appeared. Now Settings → Recipe sites lists **every site Nourish doesn't use, and why**.
  - The plan log lists them too.
  - They stay out even if you type them in as your own site.
- **BBC Good Food** gave 1 new recipe from 77 links. Nourish only looked at the first 12 recipes on each list page, and those were already saved. It now uses the whole list.
- **Diabetes Food Hub** recipes were all turned away. That site writes ingredients name first ("eggs 4.00 large") and splits names at their commas. They're now put back together and read.

## 🍽️ Plans that fit you
- **No source takes over a plan.** At most **3 meals a week** come from any one book, website, or Nourish's own recipes, unless nothing else fits a meal. You can change this in Settings → Advanced.
- **For "Lose weight", leaner and lighter dishes come first.** A rich dish that only fits by shrinking the portion a lot counts against it.
- **Budget** (Settings → Profile, the setting that was already there):
  - **Normal** (the default) and **Moderate** leave out luxury or hard-to-find ingredients like wagyu, caviar and truffle.
  - **Budget** also avoids pricier ones like steak cuts and scallops.
  - **No limit** keeps everything.
- The log says where each plan's meals came from: websites, your books, Nourish recipes, and the AI.

## ✍️ Descriptions
- **No more cut-off sentences.** A sentence that stops mid-way is never shown.
- **No made-up details.** The AI now gets the real ingredients and method. A sentence that mentions a food or a way of cooking the recipe doesn't have is rejected (pan con tomate is not a "savory pancake"), and a description made from the recipe is shown instead.
- **A recipe's own description from its website** is used when it has one.

## 🥕 Ingredients the calculator couldn't read
- **Now read:**
  - British names: swede, yoghurt, crème fraîche, courgette, aubergine, caster sugar, mince…
  - amounts in words: a handful, a knob, a small bunch;
  - "juice of 1 lemon";
  - sweeteners and brand seasonings;
  - names with commas in them.
- **Ranges** like "2–3 cloves" count as the middle, and are shown with both ends.
- **Metric amounts are shown in your units**, also inside the words ("1 x 400 g tin" → "14 oz tin").
- **A recipe is kept when only small things can't be read** (a teaspoon of something, a garnish). Its nutrition is marked **Approximate**.
- The log lists **every line that couldn't be read**, word for word.

## 🧐 Meal judgment
- Articles and guides ("How To Build a Better Smoothie", "15 Easy Breakfast Ideas") are not recipes.
- **Smoothies follow one rule:**
  - a breakfast when it has at least 250 kcal and 10 g protein a serving;
  - otherwise a drink.
- **A dish that says breakfast and is made with eggs** (Breakfast Enchiladas) is a breakfast.

## Not yet
- PDFs still can't be read on Android (EPUB and Word can).
- Scanned books depend on how clear the scan is: a faint or crooked page may give a recipe that needs review.
- Recipe sites can change or block apps at any time.
